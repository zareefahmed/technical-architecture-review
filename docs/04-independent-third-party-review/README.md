# 4. Independent Third-Party Review

This section is the heart of the guide. Its claim fits in one sentence: **when an architecture has been shaped by people who profit from what is in it, the only review you can trust is one done by someone who does not.** That means a third-party reviewer with no commercial stake in any vendor, product, or delivery contract in the solution.

The principle underneath it all:

> **"Solutions Architecture must be solutions architecture, not a sales agent of a specific vendor."**

## In this section

1. [Why Independence Matters](why-independence-matters.md) makes the structural argument. Independence is what makes a review's conclusions worth trusting, exactly as it is in financial audit.
2. [Not a Sales Agent](not-a-sales-agent.md) is the centerpiece essay: the full story behind the quote. What the sales-agent pattern is, how it happens to honest people, what it costs, and what genuine solutions architecture looks like instead.
3. [Conflicts of Interest and Vendor Bias](conflicts-of-interest-and-vendor-bias.md) is a field guide to the interests and biases around the architecture table, and how to manage them.
4. [Engaging Independent Reviewers](engaging-independent-reviewers.md) is the practical guide: how to select, verify, scope, and work with a third-party reviewer.

## The argument in five steps

1. Architecture is the set of decisions that are expensive to reverse ([Foundations](../01-foundations/what-is-technical-architecture.md)).
2. Reviewing those decisions before they harden is high-leverage work ([Architecture Review](../03-architecture-review/README.md)).
3. Many of the people offering to make or review those decisions, including vendor solutions architects, resellers, and delivery consultancies, have a financial interest in specific outcomes.
4. A review is only worth the trust you can place in its conclusions, and that trust cannot survive the reviewer having a stake in the verdict.
5. So the decisions that matter most, the selections, the vendor commitments, the whole-solution designs, deserve review by someone independent of every vendor in the room.

None of this requires believing vendors are dishonest. It only requires believing that incentives work, and every vendor's own compensation plan confirms that they do.
